The Spanish education system looks like a maze the first time you meet it.
*Concertado?* *EBAU?* *FP?* This guide puts the pieces in order — especially if
you are arriving from abroad and need to know where your child slots in.

## The ladder, at a glance

- **0-6:** Infantil.
- **6-16:** compulsory schooling (Primaria + ESO).
- **16-18:** the fork — Bachillerato (academic) or FP (vocational).
- **18 onwards:** university or higher vocational education.

## Educación Infantil (0-6)

Spain does not treat this as daycare. It is an educational stage.

- **First cycle (0-3):** *guarderías*. Usually paid, with subsidies that vary by
  region.
- **Second cycle (3-6):** **free** in state schools. Almost every child attends,
  and reading and writing already begin here.

## Educación Primaria (6-12)

Six compulsory years. What tends to surprise families from other systems is the
homework curve: light in the early years, then a sharp rise at the end of the
cycle.

**The three types of school** — this is the distinction that confuses people most:

- **Públicos:** free and secular.
- **Concertados:** jointly funded by the state and families. Many are
  religiously affiliated. The middle ground on cost.
- **Privados:** fee-paying. This is where international schools sit, with
  British, American or International Baccalaureate curricula.

## ESO (12-16): the years that decide

**Compulsory Secondary Education** ends with the *Graduado en ESO*. Without it,
later options narrow sharply.

**On repeating a year:** in Spain this does not carry the stigma it does in other
systems; it is understood as consolidating. Worth knowing, though, that the
LOMLOE — the education act in force since 2022 — restricted the circumstances in
which a year can be repeated and strengthened the support measures that come
first. If you come from a system where repeating is exceptional, ask the school
how they apply it.

## Bachillerato (16-18): the route to university

Not compulsory, and considerably more specialised than the final years of
secondary school elsewhere. You choose a track:

- Sciences and Technology
- Humanities and Social Sciences
- Arts

**The EBAU** (formerly Selectividad) is the university entrance exam. The final
mark combines your Bachillerato record and the exam, with the record weighing
more. For the most competitive degrees — Medicine, some engineering courses —
the cut-off is high and every tenth of a point counts.

## FP: no longer the fallback

Vocational education used to be the second-class route. Not any more.

- **Grado Medio:** after ESO.
- **Grado Superior:** after Bachillerato, or after a Grado Medio.

More practice than theory, strong employment outcomes, and — the part many people
do not know — **a Grado Superior can also lead to university**.

## University

Spain follows the Bologna process:

1. **Grado:** normally 4 years, 240 ECTS.
2. **Máster:** 1-2 years, 60-120 ECTS.
3. **Doctorado:** 3-5 years.

Spanish qualifications are recognised throughout the European Union.

## If you are arriving from abroad, order matters

If your child brings qualifications from another country, there is a step that
comes before all of the above: **having them formally recognised**, so the
Spanish system knows which year they enter.

And there is one mechanism that saves the academic year when the decision is
slow: the **conditional enrolment slip**, which lets you enrol while the file is
still being processed. It has small print, and we spell it out in full in our
article on the slip.
