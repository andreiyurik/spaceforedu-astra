There is a document from the Spanish Ministry of Education that solves the most stressful problem
faced by anyone arriving in Spain with a foreign qualification: **enrolment deadlines close long
before the homologation is resolved**.

It is called the **conditional enrolment slip** — *volante de inscripción condicional* — it has
existed since 1988, and a considerable share of the families who need it do not know it exists.
This article explains what it is, how to obtain it, and — the part almost nobody mentions — the
circumstances in which it can turn against you.

## The problem it solves

Homologations are not resolved quickly. Enrolment deadlines for secondary schools, vocational
programmes and universities, by contrast, are fixed and wait for no one.

Without the slip, the usual sequence is: you file the homologation, the enrolment window closes
while you wait, and you lose the whole academic year — not because of any problem with your
qualification, but because of a mismatch of calendars.

## What it actually is

**Article 15.2 of Royal Decree 104/1988** establishes that educational institutions

> may admit on a conditional basis those students whose convalidation or homologation files have
> been opened and remain pending resolution on the dates when the corresponding admission periods
> close.

The slip is the document that evidences exactly that. And its effect, in the Ministry's own words,
is that it **permits enrolment "on the same terms as if the homologation or convalidation had been
granted"**, albeit conditionally.

In other words: you enrol, you start the course and you attend class like any other student, while
your file is still being processed.

## How to obtain it

Here is the detail that makes many people lose it: **the slip is requested together with the
homologation, not afterwards**.

1. **The document is prepared** following the model published as **Annex II of Order
   ECD/3305/2002**, signed by the applicant or, if a minor, by their legal representative.
2. **It accompanies the application** for homologation or convalidation.
3. **The Registry office stamps it** when the application is filed. That stamp is what turns it
   into a valid slip. Without the stamp it is nothing.

From which follows the practically important point: **if you already filed your homologation
without requesting it, you do not have it**. And if you have not filed yet, you have a window that
closes the moment you do.

## The risk almost nobody talks about

The official text is explicit, and it is worth reading in full before relying on the slip:

> The issuing of this slip is done under the personal responsibility of the applicant and does not
> prejudge the final resolution of the file. Should the resolution not be issued in the terms
> requested, **the conditional enrolment and the results of any examinations taken** as a
> consequence of it **shall be rendered void**.

Read it twice, because it has two consequences that are not obvious.

The first is that if the homologation is refused, **you lose the enrolment**. That much was to be
expected.

The second is the surprising one: **you also lose exams you have already passed**. A full year of
completed subjects can be voided retroactively — not because you studied badly, but because the
file underpinning your enrolment did not come out as requested.

And a third, subtler point: the rule does not say "if it is refused". It says **"should the
resolution not be issued in the terms requested"**. A partially favourable resolution, or one
favourable to something other than what you asked for, also triggers the annulment.

## What this means in practice

The slip is not a shortcut or a trick: it is a legitimate mechanism, expressly provided for. But
it raises the stakes on the quality of your file considerably.

Without the slip, a badly prepared file costs you time. With the slip, a badly prepared file can
cost you **a full academic year**, including the subjects you had already passed.

So the sensible decision is not "do I request the slip or not" — you almost always should — but
**making sure the file underpinning it is properly built before you submit it**. That is precisely
the moment when a formal error stops being a delay and becomes a real risk.

## How long it lasts

The slip has a **validity period stated on the document itself**. The rule does not set a single
duration for every case: it refers to "the period stated therein". Check that date on your own
slip and bear in mind that its validity is not open-ended.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I request the slip if I have already filed my homologation?**

No. The slip is stamped when the application is filed, at the Registry office. If your file has
already been submitted, there is no later procedure to obtain it.

**Does it work for any kind of studies?**

It is provided for enrolment in educational institutions and official examinations, within the
framework of convalidations and homologations of foreign non-university studies.

**Is the institution obliged to admit me with the slip?**

The rule says institutions **may** admit conditionally. It is worth confirming with the specific
institution before relying on it, particularly with private and state-assisted schools.

**What happens if the resolution is favourable?**

The conditional enrolment ceases to be conditional and everything you have studied retains full
validity. That is the normal outcome when the file is properly presented.
