Peru joined the Hague Convention comparatively late — in 2010 — and that creates
a recurring worry: *is my apostille valid in Spain?* It is. Here is why.

## The Peruvian apostille is valid in Spain

**Peru has been a party to the Convention since 30 September 2010.**

There is a technical footnote worth knowing, because it is the source of some
confusion. Peru's accession drew objections from **Germany** and **Greece**, both
in July 2010. An objection prevents the Convention from operating **between those
two specific countries** — not with everyone else.

Two things follow:

- **Spain never objected.** For the Spanish procedure your apostille is fully
  valid.
- Both objections **were withdrawn**. Germany's was withdrawn in December 2013,
  and the Convention has been in force between Germany and Peru since 1 January
  2014.

If anyone tells you the Peruvian apostille "has problems", that is where the myth
comes from — and it stopped being true over a decade ago.

## What you need

1. **Secondary school diploma or certificate**, apostilled.
2. **Transcript of grades** for the years to be recognised, apostilled.
3. **Valid ID** (DNI or passport).

**The step that stalls the most files:** in several countries a school certificate
needs prior certification from the education ministry **before** foreign affairs
can apostille it. Check whether that applies to you before booking your apostille
appointment. Finding out at the counter costs weeks.

## Timelines

The official decision period in Spain is three months from the moment the file is
complete. In practice we see **four to eight months** for well-prepared secondary
files.

The variable is rarely the Ministry — it is correction requests. Each one restarts
the wait.

## You can enrol before the decision

The **conditional enrolment slip** (article 15.2 of Royal Decree 104/1988) lets
you enrol at university **on the same terms as if recognition had already been
granted**, while your file is still open.

It saves the academic year. But it comes with a catch worth being very clear
about: if the final decision does not come out as requested, **your enrolment is
annulled — and so are any exams you had already passed**.

It is not a safe conduct. It is a bridge, and the bridge can fall. Which is why
the file has to be built properly the first time.
