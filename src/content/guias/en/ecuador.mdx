Your Ecuadorian bachillerato does not count automatically in Spain: it has to be
formally recognised. The good news is that the paperwork route is one of the
simplest in the region.

## Ecuador has had the apostille since 2005

**Ecuador has been a party to the Hague Convention since 2 April 2005**, with no
objections from any State. You do not need consular legalisation.

## The three documents

1. **Bachillerato certificate**, apostilled.
2. **Academic record or transcript of grades**, apostilled.
3. **Valid cédula or passport**.

No sworn translation is needed: the documents are already in Spanish.

**The prior step people forget:** check whether your certificate and transcript
need endorsement or registration by the education authority **before** they can be
apostilled. A document without that step is not apostillable, and it is usually
discovered at the counter.

## The most expensive mistake, and one of the cheapest to avoid

Submitting the apostilled certificate **without** the apostilled transcript.

The file arrives incomplete, the Ministry issues a correction request, and nobody
gets those months back. Apostille both documents in the same visit.

## Timelines

Officially three months from a complete file. In practice, **four to eight
months** for well-built files. The difference is almost always correction
requests, not the Ministry's workload.

## You can start university first

The **conditional enrolment slip** (article 15.2 of Royal Decree 104/1988) lets
you **enrol on the same terms as if you already held the recognition**, with your
file still in progress. That is what saves the year.

With a warning that has to be given in full: if the final decision does not come
out as requested, **your enrolment is annulled — and so are any exams you had
already passed**.
