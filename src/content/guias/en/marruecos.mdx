Morocco joined the Hague Convention in 2016, and that changed the procedure for
the better: until then consular legalisation was required, which was slow and
expensive.

## The Moroccan apostille is valid in Spain

**Morocco has been a party to the Convention since 14 August 2016.**

One fact worth knowing if you are also considering other countries: **Germany
lodged an objection to Morocco's accession** on 14 June 2016, and it **still
stands**. That means the Convention does not operate between Morocco and Germany,
and Moroccan documents still need consular legalisation for German use.

**Spain did not object.** For the Spanish procedure your apostille is fully valid.

## The documents

1. **Baccalaureate diploma**, apostilled.
2. **Transcript of grades** for the years to be recognised, apostilled.
3. **Valid ID or passport**.
4. **Sworn translation into Spanish**, mandatory.

## Translation is the delicate part

Your documents will be in **Arabic**, in **French**, or both. Either way you need
a sworn translation into Spanish by a translator accredited by the Spanish
Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

Two concrete warnings:

- **The supply of sworn Arabic translators is limited** and lead times are longer
  than for other languages. Start looking as soon as you have the apostille, not
  after.
- **Apostille before translating.** Translate first and you may pay twice for a
  translation that is already expensive.

Also check that your name is transcribed **consistently** across every document
and your passport. Transcriptions from Arabic admit variants, and a discrepancy
triggers a correction request.

## Timelines

Three months officially from a complete file; **four to eight** in practice — and
the sworn translation can add weeks before you even submit.

## You can enrol before the decision

The **conditional enrolment slip** (article 15.2 of Royal Decree 104/1988) lets
you **enrol on the same terms as if recognition had already been granted**.

With the warning in full: if the decision does not come out as requested, **your
enrolment is annulled — and so are any exams you had already passed**.
