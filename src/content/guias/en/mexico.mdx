Your Mexican bachillerato does not count automatically in Spain: it has to be
recognised. The route is straightforward, but there is a Mexican particularity
worth knowing before you start.

## Mexico has had the apostille since 1995

**Mexico has been a party to the Hague Convention since 14 August 1995**, with no
objections from any State. You do not need consular legalisation.

**The particularity:** in Mexico the apostille is not issued by a single federal
office for everything. Depending on who issued the document, competence may lie
with the **state** authority or the **federal** one. A certificate from a state
school and one from a federal institution are not necessarily apostilled at the
same counter.

Confirm which applies to you **before** travelling. It is the mistake that
produces the most wasted trips.

## The documents

1. **Bachillerato certificate**, apostilled.
2. **Transcript of grades** for the years to be recognised, apostilled.
3. **Valid official ID** (INE or passport).

No translation is needed: the documents are already in Spanish.

## Timelines

Officially three months from a complete file. In practice, **four to eight
months**. What stretches a file is correction requests, and correction requests
come from submitting incomplete paperwork.

## Do not lose the year waiting

The **conditional enrolment slip** (article 15.2 of Royal Decree 104/1988) lets
you **enrol on the same terms as if you already held the recognition**, while your
file is still open.

With the warning in full: if the final decision does not come out as requested,
**your enrolment is annulled — and so are any exams you had already passed**. It
is a bridge, not a guarantee.
